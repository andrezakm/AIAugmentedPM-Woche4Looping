# Individual Interview Analysis: Tom Baker

## 1. Interviewee Profile

- **Name:** Tom Baker
- **Role:** Senior Sales Rep
- **Date of interview:** 2025-10-16
- **Topic:** The "Modern" Sales Process

Tom is the longest-tenured rep on the sales floor ("You've been in this game longer than anyone else on the floor"). He is an old-school, relationship-first seller whose method is built on phone calls, referrals, handshakes and golf. He sees the world through the lens of *people buy from people*. To him, the core of selling hasn't changed in 50 years and the modern tooling (social selling, multi-channel sequencing, CRM, LinkedIn "thought leadership") is mostly noise and buzzwords stacked on top of that core. Still, he's pragmatic: he admits prospects no longer pick up the phone, and he has been personally burned by not doing his homework. He is grudgingly adapting, but every new step feels like administrative overhead that pulls him away from what he believes he is actually good at: having conversations and building relationships.

## 2. Sentiment

- **Dominant emotional tone:** Grumbling, nostalgic resentment with a layer of wounded pride. He's not raging, he's grumbling. The irritation is about losing his identity as a "seller" and being turned into a screen worker and "data clerk". Under the jokes there is a real sting of embarrassment and fear of looking obsolete ("I looked like a dinosaur").
- **Energy level:** Tired-resigned but not burnt out. He self-describes as "good" and still "here", and uses humor to deflect (knees, "Influencer Tom"). He's resigned to playing a game he doesn't like ("So yeah, I have to play the game"), and not energized to change it himself.
- **Trust level:**
  - *Tools:* Low. The CRM is "always wrong anyway". He sees new methods as buzzwords.
  - *Colleagues/manager:* Ambivalent. He complies with his manager's push for LinkedIn posting but clearly doesn't believe in it ("I hate it").
  - *Leadership/the "modern" process:* Skeptical. He treats the process as fashion rather than something proven to work.
  - *Himself / human relationships:* High. He trusts referrals, handshakes and personal rapport.
- **Quote that best captures his emotional state:**
  > "It's annoying. I want to sell, not be a data clerk."

## 4. Urgency Signals

- **Research time drain, every call:** "So now I have to check their LinkedIn profile before every call." "Before every call" makes this a recurring daily cost. "Too much. Way too much." and "half the job" are the strongest intensity spikes in the interview.
- **Recent embarrassing failure:** "I had a meeting last week..." The incident is fresh (last week) and directly triggered a new mandatory behavior. Being unprepared costs him credibility in live deals right now.
- **CRM data unreliability:** "always wrong anyway" plus a concrete example (contact left six months ago). Every time he relies on the CRM he risks targeting the wrong person or company. This is ongoing friction.
- **Can't get the meeting without research:** "But you can't get the meeting without the 'research' now." He explicitly links the problem to pipeline. No research means no meetings.
- **Lower urgency:** LinkedIn posting pressure and buzzword fatigue are irritating but not framed as costing him deals today.

## 5. Underlying Needs

**What he says he wants:**
- To spend less time on screens and research, and more time talking to people ("I'd rather be golfing with them").
- To not have to post on LinkedIn.
- To "sell, not be a data clerk."
- To get back to referrals, handshakes and phone conversations.

**What he actually needs:**
- **This person needs relevant prospect context (role changes, recent articles, triggers) delivered to him ready-made before each call** so that he is never caught off guard again and can walk in with a credible, personal angle without spending "half the job" clicking around.
- **This person needs accurate, current contact data (synced with real-world sources like LinkedIn)** so that he doesn't waste effort on contacts who have moved companies and can trust the system he's required to use.
- **This person needs administrative and data-entry work removed or automated** so that his time and identity stay focused on relationship-building, where his experience is the advantage.
- **This person needs a way to stay visible to prospects who don't answer the phone that fits his style** so that he can open doors without pretending to be an "influencer". (He says he wants to avoid LinkedIn entirely, but what he really needs is a channel that feels authentic to him. Rejecting the channel is about how it feels, not about whether it's needed.)
- **This person needs to feel that his experience is still valued in the "modern" process** so that he can adopt new tools as an amplifier of his strengths rather than seeing them as proof he's a "dinosaur".

**Said vs. needed:** On the surface Tom wants to go back to the old way. But he concedes the old way no longer gets people on the phone and that research is now table stakes. His real need isn't *less research*. It's *research done for him*: the insight without the clicking. That is also what would protect him from the embarrassment he experienced.

# Individual Interview Analysis: Emily Blunt

## 1. Interviewee Profile

- **Name:** Emily Blunt
- **Role:** Marketing Manager
- **Date of interview:** 2025-10-18
- **Topic:** Lead Quality and Sales Alignment

Emily owns demand generation, and she owns the budget behind it ("my budget"). She fills the top of the funnel through webinars, whitepaper downloads and "expensive events", and she measures her success in MQL volume ("hundreds of MQLs, beautiful leads"). She sees the world through funnel volume and marketing ROI: a lead that nobody works is wasted spend and lost pipeline. Her relationship with Sales is adversarial. She describes SDRs as cherry-pickers and perfectionists who look for "the layup". She is still self-aware and fair enough to admit their point: conversion really is higher when the rep has context. She is witty and quick, and uses humor (the fake fiddle-leaf fig, "Glengarry" leads) to soften real exasperation.

## 2. Sentiment

- **Dominant emotional tone:** Indignant, territorial frustration. She feels her work and her budget are being dismissed and wasted by another team. The frustration has a defensive edge: Sales calls her leads "trash", and she counters that the leads were never worked. Under the indignation there is real grief over lost pipeline ("It's heartbreaking, really"). This is not burnout. It is the anger of someone who feels her output is undervalued and squandered.
- **Energy level:** Charged up and combative, but not resigned. She is animated, sarcastic and quick to blame. She also lets in some nuance ("I get why they do it"), which shows she is still engaged and thinking, not checked out.
- **Trust level:**
  - *Colleagues (Sales/SDRs):* Low. She doesn't trust them to work the leads in good faith and reads their behavior as pickiness, not process.
  - *Her own team and output:* High. She defends her leads as "beautiful".
  - *Tools:* Implicitly low. She knows the context SDRs want is "scattered" and calls finding it "a lot of manual correlation". No system brings it together.
  - *Leadership:* Not mentioned. There is no sign of escalation or of anyone arbitrating the Marketing–Sales conflict.
- **Quote that captures her emotional state:**
  > "We generate hundreds of MQLs, beautiful leads, and Sales says they're trash. Absolute garbage. But they aren't even working them!"

## 3. Problems They Described

1. **Sales ignores most Marketing-generated leads**
   - *What:* SDRs leave the large majority of MQLs untouched.
   - *How described:* "They ignore 90% of what we send—literally leave them rotting in the queue."
   - *Pain:* High. This is the core grievance, and she gives a concrete figure (90%).

2. **Marketing budget is wasted on leads nobody follows up**
   - *What:* Money spent on webinars, whitepapers and events doesn't turn into worked pipeline.
   - *How described:* "We spend serious budget—my budget—getting these people into the funnel."
   - *Pain:* High and personal. The stress on "my budget" shows ownership and accountability exposure.

3. **Disagreement over lead quality (the MQL definition)**
   - *What:* Marketing and Sales have no shared definition of a good lead. Marketing counts volume. Sales filters on seniority, readiness to buy and context.
   - *How described:* "Sales says they're trash"; SDRs say "this person isn't a Director level, I'm not calling."
   - *Pain:* Medium-high. It is the source of the "tension", which she calls far worse than that ("a diplomatic word for it").

4. **SDRs cherry-pick and do their own prospecting**
   - *What:* Reps skip the inbound queue and go "hunt on LinkedIn for their 'ideal accounts'".
   - *How described:* "They cherry-pick. That's what they do." "They want the layup." "The 'Glengarry' lead."
   - *Pain:* High emotionally ("It drives me crazy").

5. **Leads lack context (tech stack, buying readiness)**
   - *What:* Inbound MQLs come without the enrichment SDRs say they need. Sales says this explicitly; Emily dismisses it at first, then partly accepts it.
   - *How described:* "'Oh, we don't know their tech stack, Emily.' Well, ask them!" Later: "conversion is higher if you know the context, I admit that."
   - *Pain:* Medium for her. She treats it as Sales' excuse, but concedes it is real. This is the most important implied problem.

6. **Context research is manual, scattered and doesn't scale**
   - *What:* The context exists publicly but is spread across company pages, job descriptions, LinkedIn profiles and engineers' posts. Pulling it together takes hours per prospect.
   - *How described:* "It's scattered... It's a lot of manual correlation." "They spend hours snooping on prospects online." "It doesn't scale. They can't do that for 100 leads a day. So they do it for 5 and ignore the rest."
   - *Pain:* High in effect, even though she frames it as Sales' problem. This is the mechanism behind the 90% neglect.

7. **Pipeline is lost**
   - *What:* The end result of everything above: potential revenue never comes in.
   - *How described:* "And we lose pipeline. It's heartbreaking, really."
   - *Pain:* High. It is the emotional peak of the interview.

## 4. Urgency Signals

- **"They ignore 90% of what we send"**: a hard metric showing massive ongoing waste. Most urgent.
- **"They can't do that for 100 leads a day. So they do it for 5 and ignore the rest."**: a daily volume figure that shows the capacity gap (5 out of ~100 worked, every day).
- **"literally leave them rotting in the queue"**: lead decay. Inbound leads lose value with every day they go unworked.
- **"serious budget—my budget"**: ongoing financial exposure tied to her accountability.
- **"It drives me crazy"** / **"It's heartbreaking, really"**: emotional spikes around neglected leads and lost pipeline.
- **"we lose pipeline"**: present tense, happening now, directly tied to revenue.

The most urgent and costly issue right now: high-cost inbound leads go unworked every day because enriching them by hand doesn't scale, and pipeline and marketing ROI drain away as a result.

## 5. Underlying Needs

**What she says she wants:** For Sales to work her leads, stop cherry-picking, "get on the phone and ask them", and accept that her MQLs are good.

**What she actually needs:**

- This person needs **every inbound lead to arrive with the context Sales requires (tech stack, seniority, buying signals), assembled automatically**, so that SDRs can't dismiss MQLs as "lacking context" and can work far more than 5 a day.
- This person needs **the manual "correlation" work (company page, job postings, engineers' posts, LinkedIn) to scale across 100+ leads a day**, so that the high-converting research approach Sales already trusts can be applied to the whole queue, not a handpicked few.
- This person needs **a lead-quality definition that Marketing and Sales agree on**, so that "good lead" stops being a matter of opinion and the "trash" vs. "beautiful" fight ends.
- This person needs **visibility into what happens to each lead after handoff (worked, ignored, converted)**, so that she can show the ROI of her budget and point to where pipeline is lost.
- This person needs **evidence that her campaign spend turns into pipeline**, so that her budget, and her standing, are defensible.

**Said vs. needed:** She frames the solution as a behavior change by Sales ("ask them!"). Her own admission ("conversion is higher if you know the context... but it doesn't scale") points to the real need: a scalable way to give every lead the context SDRs need. She doesn't need Sales to try harder. She needs the context-gathering step to stop being the bottleneck. In effect, she has described the solution while blaming the other team.

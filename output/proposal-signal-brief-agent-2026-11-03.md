## In short

Your reps spend 15 to 40 minutes per prospect searching the web for a reason to call. For large accounts it takes days. We propose to build an AI agent that does this research overnight. Every morning, each prospect on a rep's list comes with a short brief: the three most relevant recent signals, each with its source, and a suggested opening. Reading it takes under two minutes. Your reps keep the judgment and the conversation, but no longer have to dig.

The engagement takes six weeks and is billed on time and material. After three weeks you see the agent working on your real prospects and decide whether to continue.

## What we heard from your team

In October 2025 we spoke with ten people across your sales, marketing, sales operations and enablement teams. Nine of them described the same bottleneck, independently of each other:

| What happens today | Who described it (by role) |
|---|---|
| About 15 minutes of research per prospect, which means only 4 prospects an hour | Sales leadership |
| 20 calls a day against a target of 50, because half the day goes into research and verification | Sales development |
| Around 30 minutes to find one personal angle for one email | Sales development |
| 40 minutes of research, often without finding a reason to call | Enablement |
| Several hours of preparation for a list of 10 well-researched prospects | Account executive |
| Days of mapping a large account before the first email goes out | Enterprise sales |
| Thorough research per inbound lead doesn't scale, so many marketing leads can't be worked in depth | Marketing |

You summed it up yourself:

> "It's all out there in plain sight. LinkedIn, Press Releases, Google News. It's not secret, it's not tucked away in some dark corner of the web. It's just unstructured. They're turning unstructured noise into structured talk tracks manually. That's the bottleneck."

Your enablement lead put the target into one sentence:

> "It's not just "is the data there?" — it's "can you find it in under two minutes?""

The data exists. What's missing is someone who turns it into a reason to call, fast enough to keep up with your territory. That is the job we propose to hand to an agent.

## What we propose

**A Signal Brief for every prospect, every morning.**

1. **Your rep's daily list goes in.** Taken from a CRM view or an uploaded list, prepared overnight.
2. **The agent checks whether the contact is still there.** If the person has left the company, the brief says so and suggests a likely successor where one can be found. No more calling people who left months ago.
3. **The agent finds the signals that matter.** It looks for the signal types your team already uses: recent funding, new hiring in engineering or operations, leadership changes at VP level, plus topics the person or company is publicly talking about. Only signals from the last 90 days, only with a verifiable source.
4. **The agent keeps the three most relevant ones** and writes one suggested opening per prospect.
5. **The brief is ready by the start of the day,** attached as a note to the CRM record and as a prioritized list: strong signals first, "no relevant signals" last.

**Example brief** *(illustrative, invented company):*

> **Jane Doe, VP Engineering, Example Corp**, still in role (verified)
> 1. Closed a Series B round 9 days ago. *Source: press release*
> 2. Three open roles in cloud infrastructure. *Source: careers page*
> 3. Joined as VP Engineering 6 weeks ago. *Source: company announcement*
>
> **Suggested opening:** New VP, fresh budget, infrastructure team growing.

If the agent finds nothing relevant, the brief says so plainly. It never invents a generic hook to fill the space.

### What the agent does not do

- **It does not send anything.** Your reps decide, write and call. The agent prepares, the rep sells.
- **It does not change data in your CRM.** It only reads and attaches notes, so nothing has to be cleaned up if you switch it off.
- **It does not replace your reps' judgment.** Every signal links to its source, so reps can check it and read between the lines themselves.

## How we would work

| Week | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1** | Kickoff. We agree on signal types and data sources, review data source permissions with your legal team, and **measure today's baseline**: research time per prospect, calls per day, reply rate. |
| **2–3** | First working agent on about 20 real prospects from your team's lists. |
| **3** | **Decision point.** 3–5 of your reps test the briefs. You decide whether we continue, based on criteria agreed in week 1 (see below). |
| **4–5** | Briefs delivered automatically every morning, attached to CRM records. |
| **5–6** | Hardening: departed contacts, missing signals, conflicting sources, source outages. |
| **6** | Live for the whole team. Measurement against the baseline starts. |

Dates are confirmed together at kickoff.

**Proposed criteria for the week-3 decision** (thresholds agreed in week 1):
1. **Usefulness:** at least [X]% of the briefs are rated useful by the test reps (suggested minimum: more than half; you may set it higher).
2. **Coverage without LinkedIn:** at least [X]% of prospects get at least one relevant, sourced signal from the agreed sources.
3. **Speed:** a rep can read a brief and decide on an opening in two minutes or less.

If these are not met, we stop and you have spent about 30 person-days.

## How we measure success

- **Target:** research time per prospect down to **two minutes or less**, measured against the week-1 baseline.
- **Also reported:** calls per rep per day, and the share of inbound marketing leads that get worked.
- **From research time to pipeline:** research time is what the agent changes directly. What your board cares about follows from it. We measure each link in this chain against the week-1 baseline, so you can fill in the numbers for your board:

  | Link | Baseline (week 1) | After launch |
  |---|---|---|
  | Prospects researched per rep per day | [baseline] | [measured] |
  | Conversations / meetings per rep per week | [baseline] | [measured] |
  | Reply rate (guardrail: must not drop) | [baseline] | [measured] |
  | Pipeline created per rep per month | [baseline] | [measured] |

  *Pipeline effects take longer than six weeks to show. We therefore report the first three links at launch and the pipeline link after [one full sales cycle].*
- **Guardrail:** reply and meeting rates must **not** drop. Speed must not cost the personal touch.
- **Rep experience:** a short before-and-after survey. The goal is that reps spend their time on conversations, not on searching.

## Data and compliance

The agent uses public sources: company websites, press releases, news, funding databases and job postings. Your team does much of its research on LinkedIn today. Whether and how LinkedIn can be used automatically depends on its terms of use and is reviewed with your legal team in week 1. The first version is built so that it works on the other sources too. The signal types your team already looks for (funding, hiring, leadership changes) are typically published in press releases, funding databases, job postings and on company websites as well. **How much value the agent delivers without LinkedIn is exactly what the week-3 decision point shows.** On request, we can show sample briefs for 3–5 of your prospects, built from these sources only, before you commit.

Your data stays yours. We use it only to deliver this engagement. We would like to agree with you whether anonymized feedback on brief quality (helpful / not helpful) may be used to improve the agent beyond this engagement. That is optional and your decision.

# Individual Interview Analysis: Jessica Chen

## 1. Interviewee Profile

- **Name:** Jessica Chen
- **Role:** Account Executive
- **Date of interview:** 2025-10-15
- **Topic:** Prospecting Habits

Jessica is the top-performing AE on the leaderboard ("Top slot again!"). Her results come from a private, unpaid operation, not from the official sales machine. She doesn't trust the pipeline the SDRs hand her, so she builds her own every night: she watches for Series B funding news, maps decision makers in a Sales Navigator licence she pays for herself, filters by how long they've been in the role (under 6 months), and researches each prospect in depth. She thinks like a hunter and a craftsperson. Timing and personal relevance ("Fresh blood. They want to buy new stuff to prove themselves") are her edge. She resents that most of her effort goes into manual data plumbing instead of the strategic selling she sees as her real job. Because she is a top performer, her workaround hides how much the official lead flow is failing.

## 2. Sentiment

- **Dominant emotional tone:** Proud but quietly resentful, with exhaustion covered by humor. She is proud of her system and her results ("I have a system", "But it works"). Underneath, she is bitter about having to carry what the company should provide: poor SDR lead quality, no tooling ("the company is too cheap"), and hours of low-value manual work. She uses jokes to deflect the sacrifice ("I don't sleep... I'm kidding. Mostly", the wine, "don't tell HR"). That reads as normalized overwork.
- **Energy level:** Charged up and driven, but running on reserves. She is still motivated and competitive. The "second shift" after her kid goes to sleep, 4 hours a night, and about 4 hours of sleep are not sustainable. She is closer to burnout than her tone suggests.
- **Trust level:**
  - *Tools:* Low trust in what the company provides. She pays for her own Sales Navigator and patches gaps with "like three different chrome extensions just to guess their email address."
  - *Colleagues (SDRs):* Low. She has given up on SDR-set meetings: "the quality... it just isn't there."
  - *Leadership/company:* Low to cynical on investment in AEs ("too cheap"). She trusts only herself and her own process.
- **Quote that captures her emotional state:**
  > "So I do my own prospecting at night after my kid goes to sleep. It's my "second shift.""

## 4. Urgency Signals

- **"4 hours!"** She says it twice, the second time with an exclamation. Daily time cost: 4 hours for 10 contacts. This is the clearest measurable pain point.
- **"every night" pattern / "second shift"**: the cost recurs daily and lands on her family time.
- **"I sleep like four hours a night"**: health and sustainability risk, half-retracted with "I'm kidding. Mostly."
- **"If I relied on inbound, I'd miss quota."** Direct revenue consequence. Without her workaround, the top performer would miss target.
- **"it's a nightmare"**: emotional spike around matching people and finding emails.
- **"I'd double my numbers."** She puts a concrete number on the upside. She believes removing the list-building bottleneck would double her output.
- **"at midnight"**: a vivid time marker that shows the work is current and happening now.

**Most urgent right now:** the manual list-building pipeline (person matching, email finding, spreadsheet entry). It eats 4 hours every night, it is the stated barrier to doubling performance, and it isn't sustainable personally.

## 5. Underlying Needs

**What she says she wants:**
- "If I could just snap my fingers and have that list of "New CTOs at Series B companies" with their emails, I'd kill it."
- Good SDR-set meetings, so she doesn't need to prospect herself ("Ideally, I'd just take meetings that the SDRs set up").
- Company-paid Sales Navigator (implied).

**What she actually needs:**

1. **This person needs trigger events (e.g., Series B funding) automatically linked to the right decision makers with verified contact data, so that** she doesn't lose hours every night on mechanical matching and email guessing.
2. **This person needs her own targeting logic (ICP: Series B, VP Ops/CTO, tenure < 6 months) built into a repeatable, shareable system, so that** her personal method stops living only in her head and her spreadsheet, and the qualified list arrives ready to work.
3. **This person needs to spend her time on deep prospect understanding and outreach, not data entry, so that** she uses her strategic selling skill, where she adds value, and feels it is used.
4. **This person needs a pipeline source she can trust (SDR- or system-generated) that meets her quality bar, so that** she can hit quota without unpaid night work.
5. **This person needs the company to visibly invest in AE tooling, so that** she feels supported rather than exploited, and her performance isn't paid for by her own money and sleep.
6. **This person needs her workload to be sustainable alongside parenting, so that** her top performance doesn't end in burnout or attrition.

**Said vs. needed:** She frames the solution as "just give me the list." The research shows she doesn't want to give up the deep personal research. She values knowing "*everything* about them." Her real need isn't a bigger list. It's **eliminating the mechanical middle layer** (match, find email, copy to spreadsheet) so her hours go to the qualitative research and personal outreach that make her successful. A plain lead list delivered without context would likely fail her quality bar, the same way SDR meetings do today.

# Individual Interview Analysis — Mike Ross

## 1. Interviewee Profile

- **Name:** Mike Ross
- **Role:** Sales Director
- **Date of interview:** 2025-10-14
- **Topic:** Lead Generation Process

Mike runs the closing side of the sales organization and sees the lead generation process from the downstream end. He judges everything by output: meetings on the calendar, phones dialing, deals closed. He is proud of his closers ("absolute sharks") and treats the lead gen team as a supplier that is failing to deliver. He is not close to the actual research work. He admits "I don't know what they do all day", and his understanding of it comes from sitting with the team occasionally rather than from data. His lens is speed and volume. He accepts the "personal touch" argument only reluctantly, because the lead gen team keeps making it. Coming from a senior seat, he has authority, a sense of urgency and an appetite for automation, but only a shallow view of the actual workflow.

## 2. Sentiment

- **Dominant emotional tone:** Impatient, skeptical frustration. It is the frustration of a results-driven leader who feels starved of input and suspects inefficiency, maybe even idleness, upstream ("I don't buy it"). After he watched the work up close, the frustration turns into a grudging partial recognition ("yeah... it looks tedious"). By the end it settles into a sense of being stuck. He names the Catch-22 himself: he wants speed, they want quality, and he has neither.
- **Energy level:** Charged up and restless rather than burnt out. He is talkative, uses vivid metaphors ("sharks", "starving", "droplets", "biography") and pushes for action ("Dialing!"). The irritation over the construction noise mirrors a general low patience threshold.
- **Trust level:**
  - *Colleagues (lead gen team):* Low to mixed. He doubts their productivity and their explanations, but he cannot dismiss their claim that the research is needed to get responses ("they insist it's the only way").
  - *Own team (closers):* High. He fully trusts their ability to close.
  - *Tools / data:* Low. The CRM attribution "Outbound" "could mean anything", so he has no reliable source-of-truth on what works.
  - *Leadership:* Not discussed. He *is* leadership in this context.
- **Quote that best captures his emotional state:**
  > "I want speed, they want quality, and right now we have neither."

## 4. Urgency Signals

- **Top-of-funnel starvation is the most urgent issue.** It came up as his first answer to "what keeps you up at night", framed with "right now" and "Always is". "Starving" is used twice for emphasis. Closers' capacity is being wasted today.
- **Low output despite significant headcount.** "A dozen bright kids" producing "a trickle. Droplets." There is an implied cost signal: twelve people's salaries for minimal pipeline.
- **Time-per-lead metric:** "20 minutes" spent reading social posts "just to write one email." This is the only concrete number in the interview and a direct throughput constraint.
- **"Right now we have neither."** He says plainly that neither speed nor quality is being achieved today.
- **Explicit call for change:** "We need to automate this, we need to speed it up." The repetition of "we need" signals pressure to act.

## 5. Underlying Needs

**What he says he wants:**
- More meetings for his closers, faster.
- The lead gen team on the phones, dialing.
- Automation and speed, without losing the personal touch.

**What he actually needs:**

1. **This person needs a steady, predictable flow of qualified meetings** so that his closing team's capacity is fully used and revenue targets are reachable.
2. **This person needs transparency into the lead gen workflow and its output** so that he can manage the team on evidence instead of suspicion and can tell real bottlenecks apart from perceived idleness.
3. **This person needs reliable source attribution in the CRM** so that he knows which channels and signals actually produce meetings and can direct effort toward them.
4. **This person needs a validated, minimal set of qualifying signals** so that research effort per lead drops to what actually changes response rates, and stops at "writing a biography."
5. **This person needs the research step consolidated and accelerated (fewer tabs, pre-aggregated prospect context)** so that personalization becomes cheap enough to stop competing with speed. That would resolve the Catch-22 rather than force a trade-off.
6. **This person needs a shared definition of a "good lead" and "enough research" with the lead gen team** so that the two teams stop working against each other's priorities.

**Said vs. needed:** Mike frames the solution as "more dialing" and "automation." His real need is not less research. It is research that is *fast enough* to keep personalization, plus the data to prove which parts of it matter. Pushing the team to dial without context would likely hurt the response rates they say depend on that context. The automation he asks for has to target the information-gathering step, not take away the personal touch.

# Individual Interview Analysis: Sam Petit

## 1. Interviewee Profile

- **Name:** Sam Petit
- **Role:** Enterprise Account Executive
- **Date of interview:** 2025-10-22
- **Topic:** Enterprise Account Planning

Sam is a seasoned enterprise seller currently working a new Fortune 500 account. He sees enterprise sales as an investigation: winning depends on understanding the full buying ecosystem (reporting lines, hidden influencers, blockers loyal to the incumbent), not on finding one buyer. He thinks of himself as a strategist and relationship builder, and he resents how much of his time goes into gathering intelligence by hand. His tools are LinkedIn (where he "lives"), dozens of browser tabs and a physical whiteboard his wife calls a "conspiracy wall." He reads his work through competence and experience: he knows what good account strategy looks like and is painfully aware that he spends most of his time below his skill level.

## 2. Sentiment

- **Dominant emotional tone:** Good-humored, self-aware exasperation. He is not angry. He jokes about the "conspiracy wall" and "In my dreams!", but under the humor is clear frustration at a process he calls "completely backwards." There is also some professional pride in being good at the detective work, mixed with resentment that the work is needed at all.
- **Energy level:** Charged up and engaged rather than burnt out. He is energized by the challenge ("It's a beast") but drained by how long the manual work takes. Fatigue is building, though he is not resigned yet: he still believes the work pays off ("I have to do it to win").
- **Trust level:**
  - *Tools:* Low trust in data quality. LinkedIn is his main source, but he treats it as noisy and unreliable ("titles are completely made up"). He has no tool that does the synthesis for him.
  - *Colleagues/organization:* Implicitly low support. "We never have the real org chart" suggests that neither the company nor the customer gives him structured account intelligence. He works alone.
  - *Leadership:* Not mentioned directly. The fact that a senior AE spends days on manual research suggests leadership hasn't invested in enablement here.
- **Quote that best captures his emotional state:**
  > "It's a waste of my experience, but I have to do it to win."

## 4. Urgency Signals

- **Quantified time loss:** "days—literal days" per account, and an explicit "80% / 20%" split between research and strategy. These are the clearest, measurable signals.
- **Active right now:** He is in the middle of a new Fortune 500 account ("I'm just trying to make sense of this new Fortune 500 account. It's a beast."). The pain is current, not something he is remembering.
- **Deal-risk language:** "the blocker who's going to kill the deal." Missing a stakeholder has direct revenue consequences.
- **Emotional spike:** "completely backwards" and "a waste of my experience." The intensity rises when he talks about how his time is spent, not about the tools themselves.
- **Unavoidable:** "I have to do it to win." He can't skip the work, so the cost repeats for every new account.
- **Cognitive load:** "connecting the dots takes real brain power" and "serious time." The cost is mental effort as well as hours.

**Most urgent:** The days of manual account mapping before first outreach. It is quantified, it repeats on every account, it is happening now, and it directly pushes out the high-value work he is paid for.

## 5. Underlying Needs

- **This person needs a reliable, consolidated view of the target account's org structure and relationships** so that he can start from a working map instead of building one from scratch over several days.
- **This person needs the scattered signals (LinkedIn connections, career history, shared employers, interactions) synthesized automatically** so that his "brain power" goes to interpreting the map, not assembling it from fifty tabs.
- **This person needs help separating real influence from noise** (inflated titles, generic posts) so that he can tell who actually matters in a deal and who only looks important.
- **This person needs early identification of likely champions and blockers** (e.g., incumbent loyalists) so that he can plan his approach before a deal is at risk.
- **This person needs a persistent, shareable, updatable account map** (instead of a physical whiteboard) so that the work compounds across accounts and can be reused by colleagues.
- **This person needs his time rebalanced toward strategy and relationship-building** so that his experience creates value instead of being spent on "low-level detective work."

**Said vs. actually needed:**
- *What he says he wants:* the "real org chart" ("In my dreams!") and to stop spending days on LinkedIn.
- *What he actually needs:* not just an org chart. A formal hierarchy wouldn't show who has "the CEO's ear" or who "used to work with Mike at Oracle." He needs a **map of influence and relationships**: informal power, shared history and alliances, plus a judgment of who matters for this specific deal. The hierarchy is only a starting point. The real value is shorter time-to-insight on the political landscape, so he can do the 80% strategy work he believes he should be doing.
- He also needs **to stay in control of the interpretation**. He takes pride in "reading between the lines." Something that fully replaces his judgment could meet resistance. He wants the legwork automated, not the strategic thinking.

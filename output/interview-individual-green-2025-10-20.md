# Individual Interview Analysis: Rachel Green

## 1. Interviewee Profile

- **Name:** Rachel Green
- **Role:** Junior SDR (Sales Development Representative)
- **Interview date:** 2025-10-20
- **Topic:** Onboarding and early struggles

Rachel is about two months into her role. She is a new SDR still in onboarding mode, working at the very front of the sales funnel. She does not own strategy, tooling or lead sourcing. She executes what marketing hands her and what her manager tells her to do. She sees the world through a newcomer's lens: everything is new, she compares herself constantly with colleagues ("Everyone else seems to do it faster"), and she tends to blame herself rather than the process. The product's complexity (API vs. SDK) is on her mind, but she has already worked out that her real bottleneck is not knowledge. It is the manual, time-consuming research needed to personalize outreach. At heart she wants to be a seller who has conversations, and she resents being turned into a researcher.

## 2. Sentiment

- **Dominant emotional tone:** Overwhelmed and self-doubting, with a layer of mild disillusionment. The frustration is not angry or blaming. It is anxious and points inward: she assumes she is doing it wrong ("I feel like I’m looking in the wrong places"). Under that is a creeping discomfort with what the job asks of her: the personalization feels "fake" and "forced", and the research feels like "stalking" and "lurking". So her values are in tension with her daily workflow.
- **Energy level:** Still charged and good-humored on the surface (jokes about walking into a closet, the sourdough story told with some pride), but it is fading. "It's exhausting" shows fatigue setting in early. She is not burnt out yet, but she is heading there.
- **Trust level:**
  - *Tools:* Low. She has no tool that helps. LinkedIn is the de-facto source ("the bible"), but she uses it by scrolling it manually. She wants automation that does not exist.
  - *Colleagues:* Neutral to admiring, but this feeds her insecurity. She believes they have figured out something she has not, and she has apparently not asked them how.
  - *Leadership / manager:* Compliant but slightly skeptical. She quotes her manager with scare quotes ("blast", "enrich", "hook"), which suggests she follows the instruction without being given a method for it.
  - *Marketing:* Implicitly low. The leads she gets are not "gold". They are raw and need heavy work before use.
- **Quote that best captures her emotional state:**
  > "It's exhausting. I just want to talk to people, not be a professional lurker."

## 4. Urgency Signals

- **Time cost per email, quantified:** "30 minutes for one email!" This is the clearest metric. At a typical SDR volume it is plainly unsustainable, and she is hitting it every day right now.
- **Scale question unanswered:** On whether this works for 50 leads: "I don't know." The workload already exceeds what her method can handle.
- **Hours lost to scrolling:** "That would save me hours of scrolling." Lost time is happening now and repeats.
- **Fatigue already present two months in:** "It's exhausting." There is an early burnout signal in a new hire, so ramp time and retention are at risk.
- **Quality degradation happening now:** When research fails, she falls back on generic hooks that feel "fake". Outreach quality is dropping today, not hypothetically.

**Most urgent:** The manual enrichment bottleneck (problems 1, 3, 6, 7). It costs measurable time on every single lead and directly limits her output.

## 5. Underlying Needs

**What she says she wants:**
- To "pull that info automatically" from LinkedIn
- A topic/intent search: "everyone who posted about Cloud Migration in the last week"
- To "just talk to people"

**What she actually needs:**

1. **This person needs relevant, recent personalization signals brought to her automatically** so that she can write a credible, personal first message in minutes instead of 30.
2. **This person needs leads prioritized by real buying intent (e.g. topic activity around what the product solves)** so that she spends her limited time on prospects with a real reason to talk, not on random personal trivia.
3. **This person needs a clear, shared playbook for what makes a "good hook" and where to find it** so that she stops guessing, stops comparing herself to faster colleagues, and ramps up with confidence.
4. **This person needs a legitimate, relevant reason to reach out** so that her outreach feels authentic rather than "fake" or "stalker-ish", which protects both her motivation and the prospect's perception.
5. **This person needs most of her time spent on conversations, not research** so that she can do the part of the job she is motivated by and is actually hired for.

**Said vs. needed:** She asks for a faster LinkedIn scraper, but her own story shows that speed alone is not the point. The sourdough email worked because it was *genuine*. The "Digital Transformation" hook feels empty because it is *generic*. Her real need is not more personal data but **relevant, credible signal** (ideally linked to buying intent, as her "Cloud Migration" example shows). She also needs the reassurance and method that her team should give her. Part of her problem is an onboarding and enablement gap disguised as a tooling gap.

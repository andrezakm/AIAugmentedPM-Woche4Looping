# Individual Interview Analysis: Sarah Jenkins

## 1. Interviewee Profile

- **Name:** Sarah Jenkins
- **Role:** Sales Development Rep (SDR)
- **Date of interview:** 2025-10-12
- **Topic:** Lead Generation Bottlenecks

Sarah is a frontline, quota-carrying SDR at the very start of the sales funnel. Her job is outbound volume: she is measured on 50 calls a day, and everything she says is filtered through that KPI. She is the person who catches the cost of every upstream failure: bad marketing data, stale CRM records, scarce tool licenses. She sees the world as a throughput problem. Every minute spent verifying, researching or reformatting is a minute not spent selling. She is pragmatic, a bit irreverent and self-aware ("Detective Jenkins"). She doesn't think in systems; she thinks in tabs open on her screen and calls not made. Because the interview takes place near quarter end, her lens is also strongly shaped by pressure to hit her numbers.

## 2. Sentiment

- **Dominant emotional tone:** Exasperated, wry frustration. It isn't anger. It is the tired, joking irritation of someone who has accepted that the system is broken and covers for it with humor ("bless their hearts", "raise alpacas in Vermont"). Underneath sits a real sense of *wasted capability*: she believes she could perform far better ("I could double my quota. Easy.") but the work in front of her blocks it. There is also a hint of identity friction. She doesn't feel like a salesperson, she feels like an investigator.
- **Energy level:** Stretched thin and running on fumes, but not burnt out or resigned. She is still fighting ("just trying to hit numbers"), still improvising workarounds. It is depleted, adrenaline-driven energy at quarter end: "running on caffeine and adrenaline since Monday."
- **Trust level:**
  - *Tools:* Low. Salesforce is "dragging as usual". ZoomInfo is "okay" but in practice out of reach. She routes around tools with Google and her own LinkedIn feed.
  - *Colleagues (marketing):* Low trust in what they produce, though not hostile toward them personally ("no offense to marketing, bless their hearts—but it's absolute garbage").
  - *Leadership:* Not mentioned directly. There is implied tension with a KPI (50 calls/day) that ignores the verification work the job actually requires, and with a decision to under-license the one useful data tool.
- **Quote that captures her emotional state:**
  > "It feels like I'm a private investigator or a stalker, not a salesperson."

## 4. Urgency Signals

- **Throughput gap of about 60% against KPI, every day:** "I'm supposed to be making 50 calls a day. That's the KPI. But I'm lucky if I get to 20." This is the clearest measurable urgency signal: a concrete, daily shortfall.
- **Half of her working time is lost:** "I spend half my day just trying to figure out who I'm supposed to actually call."
- **40% of contacts are stale:** "If they aren't, which is like 40% of the time, I have to go hunt for who the new person is."
- **"Half the emails bounce immediately"**: direct evidence that marketing's list is failing now.
- **She quantifies the upside herself:** "if I didn't have to verify every single lead, I could double my quota. Easy." She puts a number on the cost.
- **Quarter-end timing:** "it's near the end of the quarter… just trying to hit numbers". The pain hurts most right now, when revenue targets are on the line.
- **Spikes in emotional intensity:** "absolute garbage", "zombie data", "It's brutal. Seriously.", "It's exhausting", "It's a total mess."
- **Recurring tool blockage:** "the ZoomInfo login is **always** taken". This happens chronically, not occasionally.

**Most urgent:** (1) bad lead data driving (2) manual verification, which directly causes (3) the 20-vs-50 call gap.

## 5. Underlying Needs

**What she *says* she wants:**
- Better lists from marketing (less "garbage")
- To not verify every lead herself
- Easier access to ZoomInfo (more licenses)
- To make more calls / hit quota

**What she *actually* needs:**

1. **This person needs to receive pre-verified, current contact data at the moment she works a lead** so that she can spend her time on conversations instead of research. *(She asks for "better lists"; the real need is data that is current at the moment of use, not a better one-time export.)*
2. **This person needs one consolidated view of a prospect (employment status, role, recent company news, activity)** so that she doesn't have to piece the truth together across 5+ tabs and sources.
3. **This person needs data to flow into the CRM automatically and cleanly formatted** so that she doesn't spend selling time copy-pasting and fixing capitalization and spacing.
4. **This person needs to be told automatically when a contact changes jobs, and who the replacement is** so that the 40% of churned contacts become new leads instead of dead ends.
5. **This person needs timely trigger signals (job changes, promotions, PR, funding)** so that she reaches out at the right moment with a relevant hook, instead of "fishing without bait." *(She mentions doom-scrolling; the underlying need is for reliable, systematic prospecting signals.)*
6. **This person needs on-demand access to enrichment capability without contention** so that her workflow isn't blocked by a shared login. *(She frames it as a license problem; what she actually needs is enrichment available to her whenever she needs it, more licenses or not.)*
7. **This person needs a feedback loop between sales and marketing on lead quality** so that bad data is fixed at the source instead of cleaned up downstream again and again by every SDR.
8. **This person needs her performance metrics to reflect the real work, or the work to change so it matches the metrics** so that she isn't chronically judged as underperforming because of upstream data failures.

**The core gap:** She talks about volume (calls, quota), but what she needs is **trustworthy, current, actionable lead intelligence delivered into her workflow**. Volume follows once that exists.
